Our Mission

Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope is a Brooklyn-based organization that uses Folk Dance from around the world to educate and empower children and youth, helping them grow into confident, compassionate, and capable leaders. We create a tight-knit community where our students can learn, grow, and connect with others who share their values. Our club strives to inspire a love of learning, encourage respect and teamwork, and help young people stay engaged in a supportive community rather than drifting toward negative street influences.

Besides just learning how to dance, our students:

  • Become stronger and more disciplined through rigorous physical workouts
  • Learn about global history and increase their cultural awareness
  • Expand their curiosity and love of reading
  • Develop their teamwork, leadership, problem-solving, and communication skills

About the club

Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope was founded in 2016 by a family who moved to Brooklyn from Ukraine just 3 years prior. Bringing people from all different backgrounds, ages, religions, and cultures together to practice and perform was our way of giving back to the community. We teach and perform dances from a variety of cultures, including but not limited to Ukrainian, Russian, Georgian, Chinese, Spanish, Romani, Israeli, and many others.

One of the main aspects of Kaleidoscope that sets it apart from other dance organizations is the sheer amount of performances we have annually. On average, we have one performance per weekend, with some weekends even having multiple. Our dancers perform all over New York, in places such as public parks, restaurants, libraries, museums, Times Square, One World Trade Center, the Brighton Beach Boardwalk, Washington Square Park, and numerous other places. Every year, we get invited to dozens of events, such as the annual Dance Parade in Manhattan, at which we happily perform. We also travel out of state for performances multiple times per year, mostly to New Jersey and Pennsylvania, but we have also traveled to Miami for a tour and numerous performances over the course of a week, to Nashville to perform at Vanderbilt University, and even to perform at the Russian embassy in Washington, D.C.

With the support of the Multicultural Center Kaleidoscope, Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope frequently participates as a volunteer organization in annual community events. Every year, students of Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope and the Multicultural Center Kaleidoscope organize New Year’s performances open to the public. With the agreement of the parents, all proceeds from ticket sales and voluntary donations are directed toward charitable assistance for sick children and their families. There are numerous ways in which Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope engages with the community, such as the “Kaleidoscope of History” project, in which our students learn about World War II and perform war dances in honor of Victory Day. Additionally, we often perform for elderly people in nursing homes and annually offer free New Year’s themed performances across New York City. Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope does very little traditional advertising, yet the number of children participating in the Club continues to grow. Our best advertising comes from the recommendations of our dancers and their parents, as well as from our many public performances.

Our approach and methodology

At Kaleidoscope, we have developed a teaching methodology that is uniquely efficient. The primary goal that this methodology accomplishes is the development of a life-long love for learning within our students. Our unique teaching method began with folk dance, which blends movement, history, and tradition. Unlike sports or other arts, folk dance has no strict rules, freeing children from the fear of mistakes and encouraging creativity. We want to teach kids how to think before we teach them how to dance. The main way in which we do this is through our emphasis on improvisation during practices and even performances. Every day of practice, we have our youth practice inventing a dance completely on the spot, either on their own or in groups of various sizes, without knowing what music is coming or even what genre it will be. This also provides the more experienced and capable dancers with an opportunity to practice their leadership skills by directing their team to a well choreographed, fun to watch dance.

Additionally, we often incorporate basic mental exercise into our physical workout, such as having the kids answer math, history, literature, and geography-based questions or even guess a song’s nationality just by hearing it. We incentivize our students to improve their knowledge of math, history, geography, and to learn poems, as failure to answer any given question results in more physical work, oftentimes for the whole group. Our director also often tells informational stories in the fields of statistics, history, and philosophy, explaining to the youth the main takeaway in such a way that anybody can understand. As a result of our teaching methodology, we have seen our students develop critical thinking skills, leadership, and a love for learning.

Projects & Upcoming Performances

Our projects

Other than just our standard performances around New York City, Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope also engages in a few other projects in partnership with Multicultural Center Kaleidoscope.

Dance Under The Sky

Our annual street festival brings together over 20 dance groups to celebrate folk traditions and diversity in South Brooklyn. Families, schools, and communities gather to enjoy vibrant performances and cultural exchange in an open, welcoming atmosphere. This multicultural event showcases a variety of folk dances and live music from around the world, highlighting the beauty of different cultures coming together on one stage.

Summer Camp

Our immersive summer camp takes children outside of New York City to a setting where they can learn folk dances, explore new skills, and enjoy recreational activities in nature. Beyond dance, the camp encourages children to try new experiences, build independence, and discover their strengths. In a fun, family-like environment, campers grow through teamwork, cultural appreciation, and shared adventure. Each day blends structured learning with play and exploration, helping children return home more confident, connected, and inspired.

​Afterschool Program ​(Dance in Schools)

We are a DOE vendor providing folk dance and cultural programs in New York City public schools. Through our afterschool programs, students learn traditional dances from around the world, gaining confidence, teamwork skills, and cultural appreciation. Each program ends with public showcases at school events and with student participation in the NYC Dance Parade, giving young dancers the chance to perform for the wider community.

FAQ
Who can join Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope?

Children and youth of different backgrounds and experience levels are welcome. Please contact us so we can help place your child in the right class group.

Do students need prior dance experience?

No prior experience is required. We work with beginners and experienced dancers and support each student at their current level.

How do I register my child?

Use the registration section on this page and complete the form. After submission, we will contact you to confirm placement and next steps.

Where are classes and performances held?

Most classes take place in our studio on 217 Brighton Beach Ave, and performances happen across New York City and occasionally out of state depending on event invitations.

How does communication with families work?

We communicate mostly through a private telegram channel to which we add anybody who joins the club. We also use our social media pages for updates about class schedules, performances, and important announcements. For urgent questions, calling us is the fastest option.
