# Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope

## What the club is

Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope is a Brooklyn-based dance school founded in 2016 by a family who moved to Brooklyn from Ukraine. The club brings people from different backgrounds, ages, religions, and cultures together to practice and perform.

The club teaches and performs dances from many cultures, including Ukrainian, Russian, Georgian, Chinese, Spanish, Romani, Israeli, and others. It is supported by the Multicultural Center Kaleidoscope.

## Mission

Folk Dance Club Kaleidoscope uses folk dance from around the world to educate and empower children and youth. The club helps students grow into confident, compassionate, and capable leaders.

Students learn, grow, and connect with others in a close community. The club encourages a love of learning, respect, teamwork, and continued involvement in a supportive community.

Besides learning how to dance, students:

- Become stronger and more disciplined through rigorous physical workouts.
- Learn about global history and increase their cultural awareness.
- Expand their curiosity and love of reading.
- Develop teamwork, leadership, problem-solving, and communication skills.

## Teaching approach

The club's teaching method began with folk dance, which blends movement, history, and tradition. Folk dance has no strict rules, which frees children from the fear of mistakes and encourages creativity. The club teaches kids how to think before teaching them how to dance.

Improvisation is a central part of practices and performances. Students invent dances on the spot, either on their own or in groups, without knowing what music or genre is coming. More experienced dancers can direct their teams and practice leadership through this work.

The club also includes mental exercises in physical workouts. Students answer questions about math, history, literature, and geography, and sometimes identify a song's nationality by hearing it. The goal is to develop critical thinking, leadership, and a love for learning.

## Community presence

The club performs throughout New York City at public parks, restaurants, libraries, museums, Times Square, One World Trade Center, the Brighton Beach Boardwalk, Washington Square Park, and other locations. On average, the dancers have one performance each weekend, with some weekends including multiple performances.

The club also travels out of state for performances, including trips to New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Miami, Nashville, and Washington, D.C.

With support from the Multicultural Center Kaleidoscope, the club volunteers at community events. Students and the center organize public New Year's performances, and with parental agreement, ticket proceeds and voluntary donations support charitable assistance for sick children and their families.
